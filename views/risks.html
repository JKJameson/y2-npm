Understanding the Risks

Every investment carries risk, and stocks are no different. Knowing what can go wrong is just as important as knowing what can go right. This page covers the key risks to be aware of before putting real money in.

⚠
Important: Investing in the stock market always carries risk. You can lose some or all of the money you invest. This page does not constitute financial advice. Always do your own research or consult a qualified financial adviser before investing.

Volatility

Volatility refers to how sharply and quickly a stock's price moves. A highly volatile stock can gain or lose 10% or more in a single day. While volatility creates opportunities for profit, it also amplifies potential losses.

Low Volatility
High Volatility

❌ What can happen

  • A stock drops 20% in hours on bad news
  • Markets crash suddenly (Black Monday, 2008, COVID-19)
  • Your portfolio value swings wildly day to day
  • Panic selling at the worst possible moment

✓ How to manage it

  • Diversify across sectors and asset types
  • Invest for the long term. Short-term noise smooths out
  • Use stop-loss orders to cap downside
  • Only invest money you can afford to leave untouched

Fees & Hidden Costs

Fees eat into your returns over time. Many beginners overlook trading costs, but even small percentages can compound into significant losses over many trades or years.

Fee Type What it is Typical range
Trading commission Charged each time you buy or sell £0 – £10 per trade
Spread The gap between buy and sell price. You pay it on every trade 0.01% – 1%+
Currency conversion Buying US stocks with euros or pounds incurs FX fees 0.15% – 1.5%
Fund management fee Annual fee on ETFs or managed funds (also called TER or OCF) 0.03% – 1.5% p.a.
Inactivity fee Some brokers charge if you don't trade regularly £5 – £15/month
Withdrawal fee Charged when moving money out of your broker account £0 – £25
⚠ Watch out for "commission-free" brokers. Many make money through wider spreads or by selling your order flow, so you might still get a worse price than you expect.

Emotional Trading Pitfalls

The biggest risk in trading is often the trader themselves. Emotional decisions driven by fear, greed, or impatience are responsible for most beginner losses.

FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)

Buying a stock because it's already risen sharply, fearing you'll miss the wave. This often means buying at the top, just before a correction.

✓ Fix: Stick to your research. If you missed a move, the next opportunity will come.

Panic Selling

Selling in a hurry when prices drop sharply, locking in losses that might have recovered. Markets fall, but they also recover.

✓ Fix: Set a clear stop-loss in advance and don't check prices obsessively.

Overtrading

Making too many trades, often driven by boredom or excitement. Each trade costs fees, and frequent trading rarely beats a buy-and-hold strategy.

✓ Fix: Trade with a plan, not an impulse. Less is often more.

Confirmation Bias

Only reading news or opinions that support what you already believe about a stock, ignoring red flags that challenge your view.

✓ Fix: Actively seek out the bear case before investing. What would make this a bad trade?

Averaging Down Blindly

Buying more of a falling stock to lower your average price, without asking whether the company is still worth owning.

✓ Fix: Ask yourself: would you buy this stock for the first time today? If not, don't double down.

Hot Tips & Social Media Hype

Following stock tips from Reddit, TikTok, or influencers with no accountability. Many "hot tips" are pumped by people who already hold the stock.

✓ Fix: Do your own research. If it sounds too good to be true, it usually is.

✓ Am I Ready to Invest? Checklist

Before putting any money into the market, use this checklist to make sure you're in a safe position to do so:

  • ✓
    I have an emergency fund

    At least 3–6 months of living expenses saved in a separate, accessible account. Not invested.

  • ✓
    I have no high-interest debt

    Credit card or loan interest (10%+) will almost always outpace stock market returns. Pay those off first.

  • ✓
    I'm investing money I can afford to lose

    Never invest rent money, savings earmarked for a near-term goal, or borrowed funds.

  • ✓
    I understand what I'm buying

    I can explain what the company does and why I think it's a good investment.

  • ✓
    I have a plan for when to sell

    I've set a target price or stop-loss before buying, not after emotions kick in.

  • ✓
    I'm not relying on tips or hype

    My decision is based on research, not a social media post or a friend's recommendation.

⚠ If you ticked all six, you're in a good position to start. If not, take time to address those gaps first. Consider a paper trading account (simulated trading with fake money) to practice before going live.
