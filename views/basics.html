Stock Market Basics

New to investing? Start here. These are the things worth knowing before you buy your first share.

What is a Stock?

A stock (also called a share or equity) represents a small ownership stake in a company. When a company wants to raise money, it can sell pieces of itself to the public. Those pieces are stocks.

If the company grows in value, your shares become worth more. You can also earn dividends, which are a portion of the company's profits paid out to shareholders.

Capital Gains

Profit made when you sell a stock for more than you paid for it.

Dividends

Regular cash payments some companies make to shareholders from their profits.

Ownership

Owning stock gives you a small stake in the company, including voting rights in some cases.

What is the Stock Market?

The stock market is a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares of publicly listed companies. It's not one physical place but a network of exchanges and electronic systems operating around the world.

Prices change constantly based on supply and demand: if more people want to buy a stock than sell it, the price goes up, and vice versa.

Major Stock Exchanges

Stocks are traded on organised exchanges, which are regulated platforms that match buyers with sellers. Each exchange has its own listing requirements and trading hours.

NYSE

🇺🇸 New York, USA

The New York Stock Exchange is the world's largest by market capitalisation. It lists blue-chip companies like Coca-Cola, JPMorgan, and Walmart.

NASDAQ

🇺🇸 New York, USA

Fully electronic exchange known for its concentration of technology companies. Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta all trade here.

LSE

🇬🇧 London, UK

The London Stock Exchange is Europe's largest, listing over 1,000 companies including BP, HSBC, and AstraZeneca.

Euronext

🇪🇺 Pan-European

Operates across Amsterdam, Paris, Brussels, and Dublin. The main exchange for many European multinationals.

Buyers vs. Sellers

Every trade has two sides: someone buying and someone selling. Their motivations are usually opposite:

Buyers (Bulls)

  • Believe the stock price will rise
  • Looking to make a profit or earn dividends
  • Increase demand, pushing prices up
  • Often called bullish on a stock
VS

Sellers (Bears)

  • Believe the stock price will fall
  • Looking to lock in gains or cut losses
  • Increase supply, pushing prices down
  • Often called bearish on a stock
The balance between buyers and sellers at any given moment determines the price. When buyers outnumber sellers, prices rise. When sellers dominate, prices fall.

A Day in the Life of a Trading Day

Stock markets operate on a strict schedule. Here's how a typical trading day unfolds on the NYSE (Eastern Time):

  1. 4:00 AM

    Pre-Market Opens

    Extended-hours trading begins. Volume is low and spreads are wide. Generally for experienced traders only.

  2. 9:30 AM

    Opening Bell

    The main trading session begins. This is often the most volatile period as overnight news is priced in.

  3. 10:00 AM

    Morning Session

    Volatility settles. Institutional investors and fund managers are most active. High volume and tighter spreads.

  4. 12:00 PM

    Midday Lull

    Trading volume typically drops. Prices can drift or consolidate. A quieter period for new positions.

  5. 3:00 PM

    Afternoon Surge

    Volume picks back up as traders position ahead of the close. Momentum can accelerate quickly.

  6. 4:00 PM

    Closing Bell

    The main session ends. The official closing price is set. Many funds rebalance at this exact moment.

  7. 8:00 PM

    After-Hours Trading

    Extended session closes. Earnings reports released after the bell can cause big price moves here.
