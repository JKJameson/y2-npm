Further Reading

These books are consistently recommended for beginner and intermediate investors. All are widely available in libraries and bookshops.

  • The Intelligent Investor

    Benjamin Graham , 1949

    The definitive book on value investing. Warren Buffett calls it "by far the best book on investing ever written." Dense but essential.

    Intermediate

  • A Random Walk Down Wall Street

    Burton G. Malkiel , 1973

    Makes the case that index funds beat most active managers over time. A compelling read for anyone considering passive investing.

    Beginner

  • One Up On Wall Street

    Peter Lynch , 1989

    Legendary fund manager Peter Lynch explains how everyday investors can spot great stocks before Wall Street does. Engaging and practical.

    Beginner

  • The Little Book of Common Sense Investing

    John C. Bogle , 2007

    Vanguard founder Jack Bogle's argument for low-cost index fund investing. Short, clear, and genuinely life-changing for most readers.

    Beginner

  • Thinking, Fast and Slow

    Daniel Kahneman , 2011

    Not a stock book, but essential for understanding the cognitive biases that cause traders to make poor decisions under pressure.

    Intermediate
