How to Start Trading

Ready to buy your first stock? This guide walks you through the whole process, from picking a broker to placing your first order.

1. Choose a Broker 2. Open an Account 3. Fund It 4. Research 5. Place an Order 6. Monitor
1

Choose a Broker

A broker is a platform or company that executes trades on your behalf. You can't buy stocks directly from an exchange. You need a broker in between. There are two main types:

Online / App Brokers

Self-directed platforms where you make all the decisions. Usually lower fees and great for beginners who want to learn by doing.

Examples: Robinhood, eToro, Trading 212, Revolut

Full-Service Brokers

Human advisors who manage trades for you. Higher fees, but useful if you want guidance and have a larger portfolio.

Examples: Davy, Goodbody, Merrill Lynch

Tip: For beginners, an app-based broker with no minimum deposit (like Trading 212 or Revolut) is the easiest starting point.
2

Open an Account

Once you've picked a broker, you need to open an account. This is done entirely online and usually takes 10–15 minutes. Here's what to expect:

  1. ✓
    Provide personal details

    Name, address, date of birth, and nationality.

  2. ✓
    Verify your identity (KYC)

    Brokers are legally required to verify who you are. You'll upload a photo ID (passport or driving licence) and sometimes a proof of address.

  3. ✓
    Answer a suitability questionnaire

    Brokers ask about your investment experience and risk tolerance. Be honest. It's to protect you.

  4. ✓
    Accept the terms

    Review and agree to the broker's terms and conditions, including fee schedules.

Tip: Check whether the broker is regulated by a recognised authority such as the FCA (UK), SEC (USA), or CBI (Ireland) before signing up.
3

Fund Your Account

Before you can buy anything you need to deposit money. Most brokers support several deposit methods:

Bank Transfer

Most common method. Usually free but can take 1–3 business days to arrive.

Debit Card

Instant deposit, sometimes with a small fee. Convenient for smaller amounts.

Apple / Google Pay

Supported by many modern apps. Instant and easy, but often capped at lower limits.

⚠ Start small. There's no rule that says you need thousands to begin. Many brokers allow you to start with as little as €1 using fractional shares.
4

Research a Stock

Before buying, take time to understand what you're buying. You don't need to dig through complex financial reports. Start with a few basic questions:

What does the company do?

Understand the business model. How does it make money? Who are its customers?

Is it growing?

Look at revenue and profit trends over the last few years. Consistent growth is a good sign.

How does it compare?

Compare it to competitors in the same industry. Is it a leader or a laggard?

What's the news saying?

Recent news, earnings reports, and analyst opinions can move prices significantly.

Tip: Sites like Yahoo Finance, Google Finance, and your broker's own platform usually have all of this for free, in one place.
5

Place Your First Order

When you're ready to buy, you'll place an order through your broker's app or website. You need to choose the type of order:

Market Order

Buys immediately at the current market price. Fast and simple, but the exact price isn't guaranteed during fast-moving markets.

Best for: beginners, liquid stocks

Limit Order

Sets the maximum price you're willing to pay. The order only fills at that price or better. If the price never gets there, it won't go through.

Best for: when price matters more than speed

Stop-Loss Order

Automatically sells if the price drops to a set level. Useful for limiting losses without watching the market all day.

Best for: protecting an existing position

Tip: As a beginner, a market order on a well-known stock (like Apple or Microsoft) is usually the safest and simplest first trade.
6

Monitor Your Portfolio

After buying, you now own shares. Here's how to manage them sensibly:

  1. ✓
    Don't check it every hour

    Short-term price moves are normal and mostly noise. Checking too often leads to emotional decisions.

  2. ✓
    Review quarterly

    Check in when earnings reports are released (roughly every three months) to see if the company is still performing well.

  3. ✓
    Diversify over time

    Don't put all your money into one stock. Spread risk across different companies and sectors.

  4. ✓
    Know when to sell

    Have a plan before you buy. Set a target price or a stop-loss so emotion doesn't drive the decision.

⚠ Remember: Investing always carries risk. Never invest money you can't afford to lose. See our Risks page for more.
